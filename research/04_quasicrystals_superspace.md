# 04 — Quasicrystals (Physics & Materials Science) and Superspace / Higher-Dimensional Crystallography

> **Pillar in one line.** In materials science, a quasicrystal is *literally* described as the
> **3-D cut (shadow) of a periodic structure living in a higher-dimensional "superspace."** This is
> the physical, experimentally grounded instance of our project's thesis: *the tile is the shadow of a
> higher, lawful, periodic layer; the work is upstairs.* Crystallographers have been doing exactly
> this — and refining it against diffraction data — since the 1970s.

---

## 1. Overview

Classical crystallography rested on one assumption: a solid with long-range atomic order must be
**periodic** (built by translating a unit cell on a lattice). The *crystallographic restriction
theorem* then forbids 5-, 8-, 10-, and 12-fold rotational symmetry, because no periodic lattice can
carry those axes. For ~70 years "ordered solid" and "periodic solid" were treated as synonyms.

Two developments broke that identity:

1. **A purely formal/mathematical move (1970s).** P. M. de Wolff, A. Janner and T. Janssen showed
   that *incommensurately modulated* crystals — periodic crystals carrying a density wave whose period
   is irrationally related to the lattice — can be made periodic again by **embedding them in a
   higher-dimensional "superspace,"** of which the real crystal is a 3-D section. This is the
   **superspace formalism**.
2. **An experimental bombshell (1982/1984).** Dan Shechtman observed an Al–Mn alloy whose electron
   diffraction showed sharp spots with **icosahedral (2-, 3-, 5-fold) symmetry** — long-range order
   with a *forbidden* symmetry, i.e. order **without** periodicity. Levine & Steinhardt named these
   **quasicrystals**.

These two threads fused: a quasicrystal is an **aperiodic crystal** whose diffraction is *pure-point*
(sharp Bragg peaks) but which has *no* 3-D lattice periodicity, and the modern description is that it
is a **3-D irrational cut through a periodic lattice in 6-D (icosahedral) or 5-D (decagonal/octagonal)
superspace** — the materials-science twin of the cut-and-project model sets used in aperiodic-order
mathematics. The International Union of Crystallography (IUCr) ratified this by **redefining "crystal"
in 1992** as any solid with an essentially discrete diffraction diagram, periodic *or aperiodic*.

The categories of **aperiodic crystal** are three, all handled by one superspace machinery:
incommensurately modulated structures, incommensurate composite (intergrowth) structures, and
quasicrystals
([IUCr Online Dictionary of Crystallography](https://dictionary.iucr.org/Aperiodic_crystal);
[IUCr "Aperiodic crystal," Newsletter 32-2](https://www.iucr.org/news/newsletter/volume-32/number-2/aperiodic-crystal)).

## 2. The discovery, and what a quasicrystal *is*

### 2.1 Shechtman 1982 → 1984

- On **8 April 1982**, while at the U.S. National Bureau of Standards (NBS, now NIST), Dan Shechtman,
  studying a **rapidly solidified Al–Mn alloy** (~Al₆Mn) by transmission electron microscopy, recorded
  an electron-diffraction pattern with **sharp ten-fold symmetry**. Rotating the sample he found the
  full set of axes — **2-fold, 3-fold, and 5-fold** — i.e. the symmetry of an **icosahedron** (point
  group *m*-3̄-5̄). His lab-notebook entry famously reads "10 Fold ???"
  ([Nobel Scientific Background 2011](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/advanced-chemistryprize2011.pdf);
  [Nobel popular information](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2011/popular-information/)).
- After ~2 years of resistance, the result was published as **D. Shechtman, I. Blech, D. Gratias,
  J. W. Cahn, "Metallic Phase with Long-Range Orientational Order and No Translational Symmetry,"
  *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 53, 1951 (1984).** John Cahn pushed for the fast PRL publication; the title
  itself states the paradox — *orientational order, no translational symmetry*
  ([Quasicrystal — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasicrystal);
  [APS Physics, Nobel story](https://physics.aps.org/story/v28/st14)).
- Within six weeks **D. Levine & P. J. Steinhardt, "Quasicrystals: A New Class of Ordered Structures,"
  *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 53, 2477 (1984)** supplied the theory and **coined "quasicrystal"** (short for
  *quasiperiodic crystal*). They had been studying **Penrose tilings**, whose Fourier transform is
  sharp/discrete, and recognized Shechtman's pattern as the 3-D icosahedral analogue
  ([Princeton record](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/quasicrystals-a-new-class-of-ordered-structures)).

### 2.2 The controversy

The community had been told for a century that 5-fold order was impossible. Shechtman's group leader
told him to "go back and read the textbook" and later asked him to leave the group for "bringing
disgrace"; the *Journal of Applied Physics* rejected the paper as not of interest to physicists
([Chemistry World](https://www.chemistryworld.com/features/quasicrystals-scoop-prize/3004748.article)).
The most prominent critic was **Linus Pauling**, who insisted the patterns came from **multiple
twinning of ordinary cubic crystals** (five crystals rotated 72° apart faking 10-fold symmetry), and
said *"There are no quasicrystals, only quasi-scientists."* The twinning model was ruled out because
random twinning produces diffuse scattering, not the **sharp, spot-like Bragg peaks** seen; Blech's
experiments and later single-grain studies killed it
([Britannica: Daniel Shechtman](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Daniel-Shechtman);
[NPR, 2011](https://www.npr.org/2011/10/05/141087726/nobel-winning-chemist-fought-hard-for-acceptance)).

### 2.3 The 2011 Nobel Prize

The **2011 Nobel Prize in Chemistry** was awarded to **Dan Shechtman** alone "**for the discovery of
quasicrystals**." The committee said the discovery "revealed a new principle for packing of atoms and
molecules" and "led to a paradigm shift within chemistry," and noted the **IUCr's 1992 redefinition of
*crystal*** as the institutional consequence
([Physics World](https://physicsworld.com/a/quasicrystal-discovery-bags-2011-chemistry-nobel/);
[Nobel popular information](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2011/popular-information/)).

### 2.4 What a quasicrystal IS (and the redefinition of "crystal")

- **Long-range order without periodicity.** A quasicrystal is ordered (its atomic positions are
  highly correlated over arbitrarily long range) but **not periodic** (no translation maps the
  structure onto itself). It typically displays a **non-crystallographic** rotational symmetry
  (5/8/10/12-fold).
- **Pure-point (Bragg) diffraction.** The diagnostic is the diffraction pattern: **sharp Bragg peaks**
  (a *pure-point* diffraction spectrum), the same hallmark of order as in periodic crystals — but the
  peaks **cannot be indexed with 3 integers**. They require **more than 3** integer indices (6 for
  icosahedral, 5 for decagonal), revealing a higher-dimensional reciprocal lattice. Equivalently the
  Fourier transform is nonzero on a **finitely generated ℤ-module** of wavevectors that is **dense**
  in reciprocal space, rather than on a discrete lattice
  ([Quasicrystal — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasicrystal)).
- **Crystallographic restriction.** Periodic lattices admit only 1-, 2-, 3-, 4-, 6-fold rotations.
  5/8/10/12-fold axes are "forbidden" *for periodicity* — which is exactly why their presence proves
  the structure is **not** built on a 3-D lattice.
- **IUCr 1992 definition.** "By *crystal* we mean any solid having an essentially discrete diffraction
  diagram, and by *aperiodic crystal* we mean any crystal in which three-dimensional lattice
  periodicity can be considered to be absent" (*Acta Cryst.* A48 (1992) 928). This officially folded
  quasicrystals — and modulated/composite structures — into "crystal"
  ([IUCr Dictionary](https://dictionary.iucr.org/Aperiodic_crystal)).

## 3. The superspace formalism — "the higher layer is periodic; the material is its shadow"

This is the heart of the pillar. It comes in **two equivalent presentations** (the same duality our
project notes between the projection chart and the substitution chart): the **superspace-embedding /
section** picture (born from modulated structures) and the **cut-and-project** picture (born from
quasicrystals/Penrose). Crystallographers use them interchangeably.

### 3.1 Origin: incommensurately modulated structures (de Wolff, Janner, Janssen)

The formalism predates quasicrystals by a decade. **P. M. de Wolff (1974)** and **A. Janner &
T. Janssen (1977)** addressed *incommensurately modulated* crystals: a basically periodic crystal
carrying a displacive or compositional **modulation wave** with wavevector **q** that is irrationally
related to the reciprocal lattice. Such a crystal's diffraction shows **main reflections** (integer
*hkl*) plus **satellite reflections** displaced by ±**q** that need an *extra* integer index *m*:
the wavevector is **H = h a\* + k b\* + l c\* + m q**, i.e. **(3 + d)** indices.

Their move: **restore the lost translational symmetry by adding dimensions.** Treat the *d* modulation
phases as *d* extra coordinates. In the resulting **(3 + d)-dimensional superspace** the structure is
**genuinely periodic** — it has a true (3 + d)-D lattice and a **superspace group** (a generalized
space group). The real 3-D crystal is the **intersection of this superspace structure with physical
3-D space**. De Wolff, Janssen & Janner tabulated the complete list of **(3 + 1)-D superspace
groups**. Atoms cease to be points: in superspace each atom becomes a **(d-dimensional) atomic
domain / atomic surface** — a string or sheet — and the physical atom is where that string *crosses*
physical space
([IUCr, "The development of superspace crystallography…," Newsletter 22-2](https://www.iucr.org/news/newsletter/volume-22/number-2/cover-story);
[van Smaalen, "A non-mathematical introduction to the superspace description of modulated structures," *Acta Cryst.* B65 (2009)](https://journals.iucr.org/b/issues/2009/03/00/bk5084/index.html);
[Bindi & Chapuis, "Aperiodic crystals and their atomic structures in superspace," *Rend. Lincei* (2023)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12210-023-01167-z)).

> **The picture.** In superspace, atoms are **wavy strings** (modulation functions / atomic surfaces)
> that are *periodically* repeated on the superspace lattice. Physical 3-D space is a **flat cut**
> through this higher object. *Where the cut slices each string, you get a physical atom.* Slide the
> cut and the atoms shift along their modulation — that shift is the **phason** (§4). A **rational**
> cut direction reproduces a periodic (commensurate) crystal; an **irrational** cut gives an
> incommensurate/aperiodic one. This is identical in spirit to "rational slope → periodic shadow,
> irrational slope → aperiodic shadow."

### 3.2 The cut-and-project picture (Penrose, de Bruijn; icosahedral from 6-D)

For quasicrystals the same idea appears as **cut-and-project (model sets)**:

1. Take a **periodic lattice** *L* in **ℝⁿ** (*n* > 3).
2. Split ℝⁿ orthogonally into **physical/parallel space E∥** (where the material lives) and
   **internal/perpendicular space E⊥** (also called *phasonic* or *internal* space).
3. Choose an **irrational orientation** for E∥ relative to *L* (so E∥ contains no lattice vector).
4. **Select** the lattice points whose **E⊥-projection falls inside an acceptance window Ω** (the
   *atomic surface / occupation domain*, = the projection of the lattice unit cell onto E⊥).
5. **Project** the selected points to E∥. The result is the quasicrystalline point set.

- **N. G. de Bruijn (1981)** proved the **Penrose tiling is a 2-D projection of the 5-D cubic
  lattice ℤ⁵** (the "pentagrid"/cut-and-project), turning Penrose's puzzle into linear algebra
  ([de Bruijn & quasicrystals, arXiv:1306.6698](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1306.6698)).
- **P. Kramer & R. Neri (1984)** gave the **6-D cubic projection for icosahedral** quasicrystals —
  published essentially simultaneously with Shechtman's experiment
  ([Quasicrystal — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasicrystal)).
- Canonical small example — the **Fibonacci chain**: project the points of the **square lattice ℤ²**
  lying in a strip of slope **1/τ** (τ = (1+√5)/2, the golden mean) onto a line. Long (L) and short
  (S) intervals appear in the Fibonacci order LSLLSLSL…; the **irrational** slope is exactly what makes
  it never repeat. Replace 1/τ by a rational approximant *Fₖ/Fₖ₊₁* and the strip becomes commensurate
  → you get a **periodic approximant** crystal
  ([HEGL Heidelberg, "Cut-and-Project and the Fibonacci tiling"](https://apps.hegl.mathi.uni-heidelberg.de/Proseminar-WS22-Quasicrystals/pages/cut-and-project1D.html);
  [Tilings Encyclopedia: Fibonacci](https://tilings.math.uni-bielefeld.de/substitution/fibonacci/)).
- Octagonal (Ammann–Beenker) tilings = projection from **4-D**; decagonal/dodecagonal from **5-D /
  6-D**. Levitov-type stability arguments explain why precisely 8/10/12-fold tilings show up as real
  quasicrystals ([Ammann–Beenker tiling — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammann%E2%80%93Beenker_tiling)).

### 3.3 Atomic surfaces / occupation domains — the geometry that does the work

- In *n*-D, an atom is **not a point**; it is an **occupation domain (OD) / atomic surface** — a
  bounded region extended in **E⊥** (perpendicular space). Steurer: "*the atom in nD space is no longer
  a point object but an object with a bulk geometry called the occupation domain (OD) that is extended
  in the internal (also called phasonic, perpendicular) space*"
  ([Steurer, "Quasicrystals: What do we know?…," *Acta Cryst.* A74 (2018) — PMC mirror](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5740452/)).
- The OD's **shape and chemical decoration** (which element sits where inside it) *is* the structure.
  The physical-space structure is **OD ∩ E∥** at the chosen irrational cut.
- For modulated structures the atomic surface is a **smooth modulation function** (continuous string);
  for quasicrystals it is a **sharp-edged polytope** (e.g. triacontahedra for icosahedral phases). Same
  formalism, different OD geometry — this is why "modulated structures and quasicrystals are one
  family" in superspace.

## 4. Phasons — the extra degrees of freedom from the internal space

Adding dimensions adds **new physical degrees of freedom**. In a periodic crystal, the only
long-wavelength (hydrodynamic) modes are **phonons** — displacements in physical space E∥. In an
aperiodic crystal there are *also* **phasons** — displacements in the **internal/perpendicular space
E⊥** ([Phason — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phason)).

- **Geometric meaning.** A phason is a **shift / distortion of the cut relative to the atomic surfaces
  in E⊥** — equivalently a displacement of the higher-dimensional lattice points along perpendicular
  space, or a translation of the acceptance window Ω. Wikipedia: "*in the superspace picture the
  'phason' displacement can be seen as displacement of the (higher-dimensional) lattice points in the
  perpendicular space*."
- **Phonon vs phason.** "*Phonon modes are excitations of the in-plane real (parallel/direct/external)
  space, whereas phasons are excitations of the perpendicular (internal/virtual) space.*" Phonons move
  atoms *relative to the structure in space*; phasons move atoms *relative to the quasicrystal
  pattern* (rearrangements, not rigid drifts).
- **Phason flips.** Because the cut grazes the *edges* of atomic surfaces, an infinitesimal E⊥ shift
  can push an atom across an OD boundary, causing a **discrete jump** — a **phason flip** (a.k.a.
  *phasonic flip*): a local atomic hop equivalent to **flipping a pair of tiles** in the tiling picture
  (e.g. swapping the two ways a rhombus pair tiles a hexagon). These are the elementary moves that
  reshuffle a quasicrystal without changing its long-range order
  ([Phonons, phasons and atomic dynamics — ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/230762523_Phonons_phasons_and_atomic_dynamics_in_quasicrystals);
  [Widom, "Discussion of phasons," *Phil. Mag.* 88 (2008)](https://euler.phys.cmu.edu/widom/pubs/PDF/PhilMag88_2008_p2339.pdf)).
- **Diffusive, not propagating.** Hydrodynamics makes long-wavelength phasons **diffusive modes**
  (they relax by atomic hopping, decaying ~exponentially in time) — much slower than propagating
  phonons. **Frozen-in phason strain** from rapid quenching shows up in diffraction as **systematic
  peak shifts and anisotropic broadening**: "*metastable quasicrystals grown by rapid quenching… exhibit
  built-in phason strain associated with shifts and anisotropic broadenings of X-ray and electron
  diffraction peaks*" ([Phason — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phason)). Sharp, unshifted
  peaks ⇒ a well-ordered, low-phason-strain quasicrystal — a built-in **audit** of cut quality.

> For our project: the phason is the physical embodiment of *the extra internal coordinate that does
> not descend to physical space*. It is "the freedom you only see because there is a higher layer."

## 5. Diffraction theory — why projecting a higher lattice gives sharp Bragg peaks

The decisive fact: **a cut-and-project / superspace structure has pure-point (Bragg) diffraction**,
and the peaks sit at **projections of the higher-dimensional reciprocal-lattice points**.

- **Reciprocal duality of cut-and-project.** Projecting *positions* from the *n*-D direct lattice to
  E∥ is dual to projecting the *n*-D **reciprocal lattice** *L\** to E∥. Each *n*-D reciprocal point
  **G** = (G∥, G⊥) yields a candidate Bragg peak at **G∥** in physical reciprocal space. Because *L\**
  is a lattice but E∥ is irrationally oriented, the set {G∥} is a **finitely generated, dense ℤ-module**
  — hence "Bragg peaks everywhere, but countable and indexable by *n* integers." This is exactly why
  **6 indices** are needed for icosahedral phases and **5** for decagonal.
- **Intensities = Fourier transform of the atomic surface / window.** The peak at **G∥** has structure
  factor weighted by the **Fourier transform of the occupation domain (acceptance window Ω) evaluated
  at the perpendicular component G⊥**. Big |G⊥| (window varies fast) ⇒ weak peak; this produces the
  characteristic quasicrystal pattern of a few strong peaks among many weak ones, and the rapid decay
  of high-perp-index reflections. Formally: "*the structure factor consists of Bragg peaks whose
  positions are given by the reciprocal lattice of the higher-dimensional cubic lattice, and whose peak
  intensities are given by the Fourier transform of the selection window*"
  ([Steurer, "Higher-Dimensional Approach"](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-01899-2_3)).
- **Rigorous mathematics.** **A. Hof (1995)** proved that **regular model sets are pure-point
  diffractive**, with the diffraction equal to the **squared Fourier transform of the window**, via the
  **Poisson summation formula** on the underlying *n*-D lattice. This is the theorem underwriting the
  "projection → sharp Bragg peaks" claim
  ([Strungaru, "A short guide to pure point diffraction in cut-and-project sets," arXiv:1606.08831](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.08831);
  [Baake et al., "Inflation vs projection sets… the role of the window," PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7459767/)).
- **Connection to Pisot/inflation.** Quasicrystalline model sets with self-similarity have an
  **inflation factor that is a Pisot (PV) number** (τ for Fibonacci/Penrose, 1+√2 for octagonal,
  2+√3 for dodecagonal). The Pisot property is tied to **pure-point** diffraction (the
  inflation/substitution chart of the same object) — the link our project uses to bridge its two
  charts ([Baake–Grimm, *Aperiodic Order*, Vol. 1, CUP 2013 — overview](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/aperiodic-order/)).

**Intuition.** Periodicity is *sufficient* for Bragg peaks but **not necessary**. What is necessary is
a **long-range-ordered density whose Fourier transform is concentrated on a discrete (point) set.**
Cut-and-project delivers exactly that, because the order it produces is inherited from a *bona fide*
periodic lattice one dimension up.

## 6. Real quasicrystalline materials and the modeling workflow

### 6.1 Material families

| Family | Symmetry | Embedding | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Al–Mn** (Al₆Mn) | icosahedral | 6-D | Shechtman's original 1982 phase; **metastable**, rapidly quenched. |
| **Al–Cu–Fe** (~Al₆₅Cu₂₀Fe₁₅) | icosahedral | 6-D | First **thermodynamically stable** icosahedral QC (Tsai/Ishimasa-era, ~1987). |
| **Al–Pd–Mn** | icosahedral | 6-D | Stable; the workhorse for surface science, large single grains, metadislocation studies. |
| **Al–Ni–Co**, **Al–Cu–Co** | **decagonal** | 5-D | 10-fold *in-plane*, **periodic stacking** along the 10-fold axis (1 aperiodic + …; embedding 5-D). |
| **Cd–Yb, Cd–Ca (Tsai-type)** | icosahedral | 6-D | Stable binary QCs; cleanest models; basis of the Tsai cluster. |
| **Zn–Mg–RE** (RE = Y, Ho, …) | icosahedral | 6-D | Stable; magnetic rare-earth quasicrystals. |
| **Octagonal** (V–Ni–Si, Cr–Ni–Si) | 8-fold | 4-D | Dodecagonal/octagonal phases also in some oxides/dichalcogenides and **soft-matter** systems. |

- **Two structural classes overall** ([Quasicrystal — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasicrystal)):
  **icosahedral** (aperiodic in all 3 directions, needs **6-D**) and **polygonal/axial** —
  decagonal/octagonal/dodecagonal (aperiodic in a plane, **periodic** along one axis, needs **5-D**).
- **Stable vs metastable.** Early QCs were metastable (rapid quench, frozen phason strain); from ~1987
  thermodynamically **stable** QCs (Al–Cu–Fe, then Tsai-type Cd–Yb) enabled growth of large, nearly
  perfect single grains and quantitative structure solution
  ([Steurer 2018, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5740452/)).

### 6.2 Natural quasicrystals

- **Icosahedrite, Al₆₃Cu₂₄Fe₁₃** — the **first natural quasicrystal**, found by Luca Bindi, Paul
  Steinhardt and co-workers in a sample from the **Khatyrka meteorite** (Koryak Mountains, Chukotka,
  Russia); confirmed 2009, IMA-approved 2010. Its diffraction is nearly identical to synthetic
  *i*-Al–Cu–Fe. The meteorite records **shock to >5 GPa and >1200 °C followed by rapid cooling** — a
  natural analogue of rapid quenching
  ([Bindi et al., "Icosahedrite, Al₆₃Cu₂₄Fe₁₃, the first natural quasicrystal," *Am. Mineral.* 2011](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/275732563_Icosahedrite_Al63Cu24Fe13_the_first_natural_quasicrystal);
  [IUCr/IUCrJ synchrotron study, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12403171/)).
- **Decagonite, Al₇₁Ni₂₄Fe₅** — the **first natural decagonal quasicrystal**, also from Khatyrka
  ([Bindi et al., "Natural quasicrystal with decagonal symmetry," *Sci. Rep.* 5, 9111 (2015)](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep09111)).

### 6.3 The practical modeling workflow (how a crystallographer actually solves a QC)

1. **Index the diffraction pattern with *n* integers** (6 for icosahedral, 5 for decagonal) → fixes
   the **n-D reciprocal lattice** and thus the **n-D direct lattice** and superspace group.
2. **Lift** the measured reflections into n-D; the structure problem becomes finding the contents of a
   **single n-D unit cell** (periodic upstairs).
3. **Determine the occupation domains (atomic surfaces):** their **positions** in the n-D cell, their
   **shapes** in E⊥ (polytopes), and their **chemical decoration** (which element where inside the OD).
   This is the hard, under-determined part — high-|G⊥| reflections are weak, so OD edges are poorly
   constrained (Steurer's "what *can* we know?" caveat).
4. **Refine** OD shape/decoration against measured |F(G)|² (peak intensity = |FT of OD at G⊥|²),
   including **phason strain / phasonic disorder** terms to fit peak shifts and broadening.
5. **Cut** the refined n-D periodic model with physical space at the irrational orientation → the 3-D
   atomic structure (cluster models: Mackay, Bergman, Tsai clusters).
   ([Steurer & Deloudi, *Crystallography of Quasicrystals*, Springer 2009](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-642-01899-2)).

> The entire workflow is "**model the periodic object upstairs, then take the audited shadow
> downstairs.**" The fit to diffraction is the **audit** of the higher-layer model.

## Relevance to our shadow / strict-extension approach

**Superspace crystallography is the mature, experimentally validated, real-world instance of our
central thesis.** It is not an analogy — it is the *same construction*, carried out on actual atoms and
checked against diffraction data for fifty years. The dictionary between the physics pillar and our
SBT framing (see [docs/APPROACH.md](../docs/APPROACH.md)) is almost word-for-word:

| Our SBT vocabulary | Superspace / quasicrystal physics |
|---|---|
| Higher, lawful, **periodic** layer; "the work is upstairs" | The **(3+d)-D / 6-D superspace** structure — genuinely periodic, with a **superspace group** |
| **Shadow = down-projection** (the audited descent `C(U) ↓ a♯`) | The **3-D physical-space cut/section** of the superspace structure |
| **Acceptance window Ω** in the internal complement | **Atomic surface / occupation domain (OD)** in **E⊥** |
| **Internal / perpendicular space** | **E⊥** = internal/perpendicular/**phasonic** space |
| **Irrational slope of E∥** → aperiodic; rational → periodic | Irrational cut → quasicrystal/incommensurate; rational → **periodic approximant** crystal |
| Non-descending object `U` = **irrational direction / Pisot factor** | The irrational orientation of E∥; **Pisot inflation factor** (τ, 1+√2, 2+√3) |
| **Pure-point diffraction ↔ Pisot inflation** (links the two charts) | Quasicrystal Bragg diffraction; cut-and-project ↔ substitution duality; Hof's model-set theorem |
| **Shadow laws F33/F34/F35** — must factor through the torus ℝⁿ/L, must not over-read its source | Diffraction = squared **FT of the OD/window** on the **n-D reciprocal lattice**; structure factors are torus (ℝⁿ/L) Fourier–Bohr coefficients |
| The **audit** (Perron/Pisot eigenvalue test) | **Fit of the n-D model to measured diffraction**; sharp, unshifted peaks = low phason strain = good cut |

**What we can borrow conceptually:**

1. **"Model upstairs, shadow downstairs" is the proven workflow.** Crystallographers never search the
   3-D atom positions directly; they **index in n-D, solve the single periodic n-D unit cell, then
   cut.** This vindicates our decision to *walk theory space upstairs and project an audited shadow
   down*, rather than mutate shapes in the plane. The hard inverse problem (3-D positions) becomes a
   constrained forward problem (a periodic cell + a window) plus an **audit against an observable**.

2. **The window/atomic-surface IS the design object.** In QC structure solution, all the content lives
   in the **shape and decoration of the occupation domain in E⊥**. For our monotile hunt, the analogous
   lever is the **acceptance window Ω** (and the lattice + slope). "A new monotile upstairs" =
   **a new (lattice, window, irrational slope)** — exactly the projection-chart object in APPROACH.md.
   The physics literature even tells us the *grammar* of legal windows (polytopal OD's, canonical
   projection) and which symmetry orders (5/8/10/12) are realizable.

3. **Phasons formalize "the freedom that exists only because there is a higher layer."** The internal
   space is not bookkeeping — it carries **real degrees of freedom (phasons, phason flips)** that are
   invisible in the shadow's local geometry yet globally constrain it. This is the *physical*
   counterpart of our **strict-extension / non-factorization certificate (F8)**: the higher map `π₁`
   does **not** factor through the local-plane map `π₀`. Two patches can be **phason-equivalent / locally
   indistinguishable** (same π₀) yet sit at **different perpendicular-space positions** (different π₁) —
   a *fiber-collision-with-split*. **Phason degeneracy = local indistinguishability + globally forced
   hierarchy = the operational meaning of our certificate.**

4. **The diffraction audit is finite and computable.** Hof's theorem (model set ⇒ pure-point
   diffraction = |FT of window|²) is a clean, checkable signature that a point set genuinely descends
   from a higher periodic lattice. It is the physical sibling of our demand for a **finite, computable
   certificate** that the shadow "came from a higher layer rather than being a within-plane artifact."
   The Pisot ↔ pure-point linkage is precisely the **F41 duality** bridge between our projection chart
   and substitution chart.

5. **Approximants are the rational-slope control.** Rational cuts give **periodic approximants** with
   the *same local clusters* but true periodicity — the materials-science demonstration that
   **rational slope → periodic shadow** and that aperiodicity is a **between-layer** effect of the
   irrational direction, not a within-plane property. This is direct empirical support for the primer's
   anti-reductionism point that motivated retiring the plane-search engine.

**Caveat to import honestly.** Steurer's "what *can* we know?" warns that **OD shapes are
under-determined** by data (high-perp-index peaks are weak). The analogue for us: a candidate higher
layer may be **only weakly constrained by its shadow's local statistics**, so the **certificate
(F8/F33–F35)** — not mere visual/local agreement — must be the arbiter that a shadow truly has
higher-layer origin.

> **Bottom line.** Quasicrystals are the universe's existence proof that *ordered ⊋ periodic*, and
> superspace crystallography is the worked, audited engineering of "tile = shadow of a higher periodic
> layer." Our project is, in effect, **running superspace crystallography in reverse and in theory
> space**: instead of solving the upstairs cell that fits a *given* material, we **walk to an
> upstairs (lattice, window, irrational slope) whose audited shadow is a *new* aperiodic monotile.**
