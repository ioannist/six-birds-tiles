## The SBT mechanism in concrete terms

The lower carrier is the space of **bare geometric tilings**. The richer carrier
retains contextual roles and an entire sequence of recognized scales. R001
exhibits an unwanted failure of descent: the proposed unbounded separated
hierarchy cannot be recovered equivariantly from a periodic bare tiling.
R002 gives a carrier-local saturation witness for one attempted repair class.

For the hat, the actual boundary and its neighbourhoods repair this geometric
interface. The necessary contextual readout descends by the recognition theorem.
A hypothetical nonzero period would then have to descend consistently through
all increasingly separated scales, which the positive geometric bound forbids.
This is the **useful** obstruction: the periodic quotient cannot carry the
recognized structure. It should not be confused with the **unwanted** obstruction
that a proposed solid has no tilings or cannot implement its labels.

P1 chooses a convenient grid/gauge; P2 proves placement legality and existence;
P3 transports symmetries and checks interface compatibility; P4 builds the
varying-scale tower; P5 retains precisely the contextual roles or finite fibers
needed for recognition; P6 certifies positive separation and closes the return.
These are concrete mathematical operations, not labels substituted for proofs.

The quantitative Xi control in sbt_einstein/review_2026-09-06 remains useful for
legal translation probes: uncoupled/sheared channels can leave a blind direction.
Here the inball/tower theorem supplies the nonlinear geometric anti-periodicity
bridge. No arbitrary shape-fit score is being identified with Xi.

## The theorem extensions earned by calibration

R004 proves period exclusion across changing geometric levels. It is dimension
independent and includes an abstract Lean implementation. R006 generalizes a
canonical readout to a finite equivariant set of global readouts, with an explicit
ambiguity budget. Both are derived ordinary mathematical results usable as SBT
adapters; neither asserts a novel priority claim nor assumes a desired solid
into existence. Further theorem extensions remain legitimate construction work.

## Next concrete 3D packet

Use the known 2D hat as a forcing control, then explicitly expose the remaining
vertical translation of its unit-height prism stack. The horizontal hierarchy
is real, yet every slab height stays one: a nonzero vertical period survives.
This is a specific residual for coupling design, not evidence against all 3D
monotiles. It is also a direct geometric counterpart of the existing linear
xy-only residual control.

Then construct one connected 3D module whose contacts force a second hierarchy
or an audited vertical phase relation. Require an actual nonoverlapping extending
patch, a geometric decoder on every allowed local configuration, and a proof
that the second channel sees the surviving vertical direction. Keep finite
global ambiguity if it avoids artificial uniqueness constraints. A height lock
that merely enforces identical slabs is a negative control: it retains the
unit vertical period. Simple extrusion is therefore a starting witness, not
a completed 3D Einstein.

Any design must also respect the earlier repository obstructions: fixed-metric
ABCK finite-offset recovery (step281), nonzero Dehn/edge-fan failures of earlier
supports, and all aligned rho-class exclusions of the current S solid. Those
results remain scoped to their actual carriers. Nothing in the 2D calibration
reopens an unchanged excluded 3D construction.

For screw symmetries, a separate finite-orientation/rotation-order bridge is
needed: a screw with finite-order rotation and nonzero axial displacement has
a nonzero translational power. Full-dimensional aperiodicity plus that bridge
excludes it. Do not treat a finite set of orientations in a constructed sample
as a theorem about every tiling.
